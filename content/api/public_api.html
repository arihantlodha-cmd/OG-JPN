OG-JPN API#

The ogjpn package is the Japan calibration layer. Each module returns parameters in the form OG-Core’s Specifications.update_specifications accepts.

ogjpn.calibrate#

OG-Japan (OG-JPN) calibration.

The Calibration class assembles the country-specific pieces (macro parameters, tax parameters, and demographics) into a single dict that can be handed to an OG-Core Specifications object via update_specifications. This mirrors the Calibration classes in the other country models (OG-USA, OG-THA, OG-BRA).

Phase status:

  • macro parameters: provisional first pass (ogjpn.macro_params)

  • tax parameters: progressive labor income tax fitted to NTA data, an effective capital income tax from the national accounts, and payroll, corporate, and consumption taxes from published Japanese rates (ogjpn.tax_params)

  • demographics: live UN data by country code 392, requires a UN API token (see README); optional so the pipeline still runs without it

ogjpn.macro_params#

OG-Japan macro calibration.

Sourced from the IMF World Economic Outlook via the IMF DataMapper API (retrieved 2026-08-18). Japan’s general government, share of GDP:

  • gross debt 214.5% (2024, GGXWDG_NGDP)

  • total revenue 37.6% (2024, indicator “rev”)

  • total expenditure 39.1% (2024, indicator “exp”)

  • net lending/borrowing -1.7% (2024, GGXCNL_NGDP)

  • real GDP growth ~0.7% (2023, NGDP_RPCH)

These pin the debt ratio, the growth rate, and the overall size of government. The split of total expenditure (39.1%) into its OG-Core pieces – government consumption (alpha_G), pensions (a separate model outlay), non-pension transfers (alpha_T), interest, and investment – is estimated from the standard composition, since the aggregate API series do not break it out. gamma (capital share) is from the Penn World Table labor share (see get_macro_params).

ogjpn.tax_params#

OG-Japan tax calibration.

Japan has no open-source tax microsimulation model (no equivalent of the US Tax-Calculator), so taxes are calibrated from administrative data and published rates:

  • Personal income tax (default, income_tax="progressive"): Japan’s progressive schedule on labor income, fitted to the National Tax Agency’s 2023 salary-bracket data, plus a flat effective tax on capital income, combined age by age into OG-Core’s DEP_totalinc form (see ogjpn.income_tax). The option income_tax="linear" keeps the earlier constant rates (8% average, 30% marginal on labor, 20.315% on capital).

  • Social insurance (payroll on labor): about 30% of remuneration (Japan Pension Service / MHLW): pension 18.3%, health ~10%, long-term care ~0.8% (blended), employment ~0.9%, employer and employee sides both borne by labor. OG-Core’s payroll tax revenue funds general government rather than an earmarked pension pot. The contribution cap is not modeled.

  • Corporate income: effective combined rate about 30% (national 23.2% plus local corporate, inhabitant, and enterprise taxes) (PwC / JETRO).

  • Consumption tax: 10% since October 2019 (National Tax Agency / MOF).

Japan’s property tax (Fixed Asset plus City Planning Tax, ~2% of GDP) could be added through OG-Core’s wealth tax but made the consumption-tax reform fail to converge, so it is deferred.

ogjpn.income_tax#

Japan’s progressive personal income tax, for OG-Core’s DEP_totalinc tax functions.

Effective rates come from administrative data, not a tax simulator. The National Tax Agency’s 2023 salary survey (民間給与実態統計調査, see scripts/fetch_nta_tax_data.py) reports, for each salary bracket, the number of earners, total salary, number of taxpayers, and national income tax withheld. From these:

  • National income tax per bracket is the tax withheld (it includes the 2.1% reconstruction surtax).

  • Resident tax (住民税, 10% of taxable income plus a flat per-capita levy) is not withheld by employers, so it is built from taxable income. Taxable income per taxpayer is recovered by inverting the national bracket schedule on tax withheld per taxpayer; resident taxable income is higher by the 50,000 yen gap between the national (480,000) and resident (430,000) basic deductions.

  • Effective rate = (national + resident tax) / salary, over all earners in the bracket, taxpayers or not.

Marginal rates on labor income are statutory, evaluated at each bracket’s mean salary: (national bracket rate x 1.021 + 10% resident) x (1 - slope of the employment income deduction - employee social insurance rate), scaled by the share of the bracket that pays tax.

Both are fitted with OG-Core’s DEP_totalinc form,

rate(I) = (max - min) * (A I^2 + B I) / (A I^2 + B I + 1) + min,

on income expressed relative to mean salary, then rescaled to the model’s income anchor (mean_income_data), so the progressivity relative to the mean is right whatever currency the anchor is in.

Labor and capital income are taxed separately in Japan: salaries on the progressive schedule, most financial income at a flat rate. OG-Core’s DEP_totalinc form takes total income I, but its parameters can vary by age. At age s with labor share of income lambda_s, the separable tax lambda tau_L(lambda I) + (1 - lambda) tau_k is again a DEP_totalinc curve (age_specific_params), so each age gets its own curve. The labor shares come from the model’s own steady state (labor_share_by_age, iterated to a fixed point by scripts/calibrate_labor_share.py and stored in data/labor_share_by_age.csv).

The capital rate tau_k is an effective rate, not the 20.315% statute: Japan taxes interest, dividends, and investment-trust income at 20.315%, but leaves untaxed the return on owner-occupied housing and the investment income of insurance and pension reserves, which together are most of household capital income (effective_capital_tax_rate).

Limits: the survey covers private-sector salary earners only; deductions other than the basic deduction are averaged into the inversion; the labor share is an average over lifetime-income groups at each age; realized capital gains, rental income (taxed progressively), and NISA exemptions are left out of the capital rate.

ogjpn.income_tax.age_specific_params(etr, mtrx, labor_share, tau_k, mean_income)[source]#

DEP_totalinc parameters by age for a separable labor/capital tax.

With labor share lambda at an age, labor income is lambda I, so the tax on labor is lambda I tau_L(lambda I) and on capital (1 - lambda) I tau_k. Dividing by I, the effective rate is lambda tau_L(lambda I) + (1 - lambda) tau_k: a DEP_totalinc curve with A and B scaled by lambda^2 and lambda, and the top and bottom rates by lambda plus the constant capital term. The marginal rate on labor is the labor schedule at lambda I (A and B scaled, rates unchanged); the marginal rate on capital is tau_k.

Parameters:

  • etr (array_like) – A, B, max, min of the labor effective rate on income relative to mean salary (fit_dep_totalinc)

  • mtrx (array_like) – same for the labor marginal rate

  • labor_share (array_like) – labor share of income at each age

  • tau_k (float) – flat tax rate on capital income

  • mean_income (float) – model income at the mean salary

Returns:

etr, mtrx, mtry parameters, each a list of S lists of six

OG-Core DEP_totalinc coefficients

Return type:

(tuple)

ogjpn.pension_params#

Japan’s public pension formula and the yen income anchor.

Japan’s public pension has two tiers:

  1. Basic pension (国民年金・老齢基礎年金), a flat amount for 40 years of contributions: 795,000 yen a year in FY2023 (Japan Pension Service, 令和5 年度, new recipients aged 67 or under).

  2. Employees’ pension (厚生年金・報酬比例部分), proportional to earnings: annual benefit = average indexed standard remuneration x 5.481/1000 x months of coverage (the post-April-2003 rate, bonuses included). Over a 40-year career that is 0.005481 x 40 = 21.9 percent of average earnings.

OG-Core’s “US-Style Social Security” system computes a monthly benefit (PIA) from average indexed monthly earnings (AIME) with three bracket rates through the origin. Setting the second and third rates to 21.9 percent and placing the first bend point where 90 percent of AIME equals the basic pension plus 21.9 percent of AIME reproduces Japan’s formula exactly above the bend point:

PIA = 0.9 b1 + 0.219 (AIME - b1) = basic + 0.219 AIME, AIME >= b1,

with b1 = basic / (0.9 - 0.219). Below it, the minimum payment (the basic pension) is a floor. Benefits are capped at the basic pension plus 21.9 percent of the contribution ceiling (standard monthly remuneration of 650,000 yen plus a 1.5 million yen bonus a year).

The formula is in yen, so it needs the model’s income anchor in yen: mean_income_data is set to the mean salary of full-year private-sector earners in 2023 (NTA 民間給与実態統計調査, 4.595 million yen), the same data year and source as the income-tax calibration (ogjpn.income_tax).

Limits: the spouse’s basic pension (第3号被保険者) and survivor benefits are not modeled; AIME averages the best 35 years (OG-Core’s avg_earn_num_years) rather than all months of coverage; and the macroeconomic slide (マクロ経済スライド) is a policy experiment (docs results), not part of the baseline formula.

ogjpn.reporting#

Helpers for reporting reform transitions.

OG-Core holds government purchases at a fixed share of GDP until the debt-closure rule starts (period tG1, 20 by default). A reform that raises revenue or cuts spending therefore pays down government debt over those first 20 years. Lower debt frees household saving for private capital (“crowding in”), so capital rises along the transition even when households save less. After tG1 the closure rule brings debt back to its target and capital returns toward its new steady state. These helpers measure that channel so the write-ups state it from the solved paths.

ogjpn.wealth#

Japan’s observed initial distribution of household wealth, for the start of the transition path.

By default OG-Core starts a baseline transition from the terminal steady state’s wealth-by-age profile, rescaled to the initial population. For Japan that proxy is poor: today’s population is far older than the terminal stable population, so the steady-state profile puts wealth in the wrong cohorts, and the mismatch showed up as a one-period seam in the resource constraint at t=2 (see docs/NEXT_STEPS.md, item 4).

OG-Core (PR #1254) lets a country model supply initial_hhwealth_dist, an S x J matrix of shares of aggregate household wealth. This module builds it from Japanese survey data:

  • Age profile (data). Mean household net worth (家計資産総額, which is the 純資産総額 of the statistical tables: net financial assets plus housing and residential land) by age of household head, all households, from the 2019 National Survey of Family Income, Consumption and Wealth (2019年全国家計構造調査), Statistics Bureau of Japan, 結果の概要 (2021-05-18), Figure IV-4.

  • Within-age split across lifetime-income groups (model). The survey summary does not cross age with lifetime income, so the split across the J groups at each age is taken from the model’s own steady state. Only the age profile is replaced by data.

Known limits, stated rather than hidden:

  • The survey reports wealth per household by the age of the head; the model is per person. Assigning household wealth to the head’s age overstates wealth at young ages slightly (adult children living with their parents hold little but are counted at their own age in the model) and is the standard first-pass mapping.

  • The survey uses age bands (under 30, 30s, …, 80 and over). The profile is interpolated linearly between band midpoints and held flat beyond the first and last midpoints.

  • Only the shape is taken from the data. The level of aggregate wealth is still the steady-state level unless initial_wealth_ratio is also set.
